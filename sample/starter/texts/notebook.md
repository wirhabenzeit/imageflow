## What to remember

The red boathouses looked small against the mountains. From the road they felt like the only lights for miles, though we had passed two other houses a few minutes earlier. A pile of wood stood against the wall. We took one picture and kept walking.

The shore was mostly stone under snow. A line of ice marked the earlier tide, and tiny pools had frozen into plates of grey glass. We stood back from the water and watched the wind turn the surface from smooth steel to a scattering of bright points.

1. Follow the old road past the bend.
2. Stop for a view of the ridge before the path turns inland.
3. Leave enough time to walk back while there is light.

The useful notes were mostly about where to stand and when to come back. The rest was already in the pictures.

## On the light

Light this far north in March does not behave the way it does at home. The sun stays low and sideways all day, so that every rock throws a long shadow and the snow on the slopes turns from white to blue to rose within an hour. Photographs taken at four in the afternoon look like dawn. Photographs taken an hour later look like dusk, because they are.

We learned to plan the days around the light. Lunch happened when the sun reached the ridge, and the walk back happened when it went flat. On cloudy days everything softened and the colours went quiet, and those turned out to be some of the best pictures: grey water, grey sky, and a single red boathouse in between. On the clear nights, if we were lucky, the sky itself began to move.

## Small things

- The smell of woodsmoke on the cold air.
- A sheep standing in the snow beside an old turf shed.
- The ferry horn, twice a day, carrying across the whole fjord.
- Green light above the ridge, there for twenty minutes and gone.
- How quiet it was at the top of the ridge once the wind dropped.

None of these made a good photograph on its own. Together they are the reason the photographs mean anything at all.

## For next time

Bring a second pair of gloves, because one pair never dries. Bring a lighter lens for long walks, and leave the heavy one in the car more often. Write the notes on the same day, before the details blur into each other. And plan one day with no plan at all, because the best afternoon of the trip was the one we had kept free for bad weather that never came.
