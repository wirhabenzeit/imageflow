We reached the coast in the long light of evening. Across the water, the ridge rose straight from the fjord. Cloud shadows moved across its face while the village below stayed bright. We left our bags in the car and followed a strip of gravel down to the landing, where a small boat tugged at its rope.

The house we had rented stood a little above the road, with a bench by the door and a view straight down the fjord. Someone had left a jar of wildflowers on the table and a note about the stove. We ate bread and cheese at the window and watched the light refuse to fade. Close to midnight the sky was still the colour of weak tea, and the ridge across the water had turned a soft, even blue.

The next morning the path above Fjordgård was wet. Each turn opened a different view of the same water. Sometimes it looked grey, sometimes nearly green. At the edge of the birches we stopped to listen. The sound of the road was already gone, though the village was still close enough to see.

Higher up, the birches thinned into heather and the path became a line of worn stones. We climbed slowly, stopping often, partly for breath and partly because every few metres the fjord looked new again. A pair of ravens kept pace with us along the slope, calling to each other and drifting out over the drop. Near the top the wind arrived all at once, cold and steady from the open sea.

We did not go all the way to the summit. A low shelf of rock just below it gave us the whole view: the village, the boats, the outer islands lying flat on the water, and far to the north a line of peaks with snow still in their gullies. We ate our sandwiches with our backs to the wind and took turns walking to the edge with the camera.

After lunch we tried the other side of the valley. The trail crossed a stream and passed through a low stand of birches. Where the trees ended, the slope opened onto shelves of bare stone. We sat there for a while, trading the camera back and forth as the light moved between the clouds.

## Changing weather

In the afternoon the clouds came down over the ridge like a lid. Within half an hour the far shore had disappeared, and the water in front of us went from blue to pewter. We walked back along the shore road in a fine rain that seemed to hang in the air rather than fall. The few cars that passed slowed down to let us step onto the verge, and one driver raised a hand from the wheel as if we were neighbours.

By the harbour the fishing boats sat low and quiet. A man in orange overalls was coiling rope on the quay and nodded without stopping. Gulls stood in a row along the roof of the shed, all facing the same way into the weather. We bought coffee from the small shop by the fuel pump and drank it standing in the doorway, watching the rain pass across the water in grey curtains.

*By the time we turned back, the fjord had gone almost still.* At the landing, the same boat was there and the rope had stopped pulling. We took the long way to the car and watched a row of lights come on across the water.
