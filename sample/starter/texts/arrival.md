---
title: Arriving
---

We reached the coast in the last light of the afternoon. The mountains stood in blue shadow, but a band of sun still lay across the ridge above the village, and the snow on it was the colour of the inside of a shell. We left our bags in the car and walked down to the landing, where a small boat tugged at its rope and a thin skin of ice had formed along the edge of the quay.

The house we had rented stood a little above the road, with a snow-covered bench by the door and a view straight down the fjord. Someone had lit the stove for us and left a note about the damper. We ate bread and cheese at the window and watched the last colour leave the sky. By nine the ridge across the water had gone a deep, even blue, and the first stars were out above it.

The next morning the path above the village was hard with frost. Each turn opened a different view of the same water, which looked black under the cliffs and silver where the sun reached it. At the edge of the birches we stopped to listen. The sound of the road was already gone, though the village was still close enough to see.

Higher up, the birches thinned into open snow and the path became a line of footprints left by someone before us. We climbed slowly, stopping often, partly for breath and partly because every few metres the fjord looked new again. A pair of ravens kept pace with us along the slope, calling to each other and drifting out over the drop. Near the top the wind arrived all at once, cold and steady from the open sea.

We did not go all the way to the summit. A shelf of rock just below it gave us the whole view: the village, the boats, the outer islands lying flat on the water, and far to the north a line of peaks with the sun low behind them. We ate our sandwiches with our backs to the wind and took turns walking to the edge with the camera.

After lunch we tried the other side of the valley. The trail crossed a frozen stream and passed through a low stand of birches. Where the trees ended, the slope opened onto shelves of wind-packed snow. We sat there for a while, trading the camera back and forth as the light moved between the clouds.

## Changing weather

In the afternoon the clouds came down over the ridge like a lid. Within half an hour the far shore had disappeared, and the water in front of us went from blue to pewter. We walked back along the shore road in a fine snow that seemed to hang in the air rather than fall. The few cars that passed slowed down to let us step onto the verge, and one driver raised a hand from the wheel as if we were neighbours.

By the harbour the fishing boats sat low and quiet. A man in orange overalls was clearing snow from the quay and nodded without stopping. Gulls stood in a row along the roof of the shed, all facing the same way into the weather. We bought coffee from the small shop by the fuel pump and drank it standing in the doorway, watching the snow pass across the water in grey curtains.

*By the time we turned back, the fjord had gone almost still.* At the landing, the same boat was there and the rope had stopped pulling. We took the long way to the car and watched a row of lights come on across the water.
